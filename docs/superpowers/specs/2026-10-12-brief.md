  2. Write 12 modules in parallel (one agent per module, each produces reading content + a question bank, validated against the schema).
  3. Build the final-exam bank by merging all module banks and adding cross-module questions (one agent).

## Who it's for

Poppy — an NHS dietitian with no insurance background, meeting the head of a health broking team in ~2 weeks about a possible move into broking. She needs a plain-English grounding in what insurance is and how the UK market works, at roughly CII IF1 (Insurance, Legal & Regulatory) level, plus a basic understanding of health insurance. She'll study on her **phone**, in short sessions, after work.

## Tone

Relaxed, warm, plain English. Written *for* Poppy, not for an exam board. Use everyday examples (car, home, travel, phone) and, where it helps, clinical/NHS analogies she already understands (triage, consent, duty of care, referral pathways). No jargon without an immediate plain definition. Short paragraphs. Header on the home screen: **"Insurance Foundations — built for Poppy"**.

All content must be **original**. Cover the IF1 syllabus topics; do not reproduce or closely paraphrase CII study text.

## Learning design

- **12 modules**, each: reading (~2,000 words, split into 4–6 sections with a one-line "why this matters for a broker" note at the end) → **10-question quiz**.
- Each module has a **bank of 30 questions**; each quiz attempt draws **10 at random**, shuffles answer order.
- **Pass mark 8/10** to unlock the next module. **Unlimited retries.** A fresh draw each retry.
- Instant feedback per question: correct/incorrect + a 1–2 sentence explanation. Score summary at the end with "retry" / "next module".
- **Final exam**: unlocked when all 12 modules are passed. **100 questions drawn at random from a bank of ~250** (the 12 × 30 module questions = 360 minus overlap, plus ~40 cross-module scenario questions; de-duplicate so the bank is ≥250 distinct questions). Pass mark **70%**. Untimed, but show elapsed time. Results screen breaks the score down by module so she can see weak areas and jump back to them. Can be retaken unlimited times.
- A **"Quick recap"** card at the top of each module (5 bullet key points) so she can revise fast before the exam.

## Modules (IF1-aligned)

Each agent gets its module number, title and the topic list below, and must cover every topic.

1. **What insurance actually is** — risk (pure vs speculative, fundamental vs particular), peril vs hazard, risk transfer, pooling/law of large numbers, insurable vs uninsurable risks, frequency & severity, why people and businesses buy cover, the premium as the price of transfer.
2. **How the UK insurance market works** — insurers (composite, specialist, mutuals), Lloyd's (syndicates, managing agents, how placing works at a high level), brokers vs agents vs MGAs vs comparison sites, reinsurance in one paragraph, the broker's role and how brokers are paid (commission, fees), wholesale vs retail, big-name players so she recognises them.
3. **Contract basics & agency** — what makes a contract (offer, acceptance, consideration, intention, capacity), how an insurance contract forms (proposal, quote, acceptance, cover note, policy), agency law in plain terms (who the broker acts for, authority, duties to client), cancellation & renewal.
4. **Insurable interest** — definition, why it exists, when it must exist (life vs property vs liability), common examples, what happens without it.
5. **Fair presentation & good faith** — Insurance Act 2015 (commercial) and Consumer Insurance (Disclosure and Representations) Act 2012 (consumer), material facts, duty of fair presentation, what counts as a reasonable search, remedies for breach (deliberate/reckless vs careless, proportionate remedies), misrepresentation, the broker's role in getting disclosure right.
6. **Proximate cause** — definition, chains of events, concurrent and sequential causes, how exclusions interact, worked examples (storm → power cut → freezer contents; fire → water damage).
7. **Indemnity** — principle and purpose, how it's measured (market value, reinstatement, new-for-old, agreed value), methods of providing it (cash, repair, replace, reinstate), excesses/deductibles, limits, average/underinsurance, betterment, policies that aren't indemnity (benefit policies — bridge to health/protection).
8. **Contribution & subrogation** — dual insurance and how contribution works (rateable proportion in simple terms), subrogation (why and how insurers recover from third parties), waiver of subrogation, how these support indemnity.
9. **Policies, wordings & claims** — structure of a policy (schedule, operative clause, conditions, exclusions, warranties, endorsements), claims process end to end (notification, evidence, adjusters, settlement, complaints), what "claims-made" vs "losses-occurring" means, renewal and claims experience.
10. **Regulation, FCA & Consumer Duty** — who regulates what (FCA conduct, PRA prudential), FCA Principles for Businesses, Consumer Duty in plain terms (good outcomes, price & value, understanding, support), ICOBS basics, Financial Ombudsman Service, FSCS, Treating Customers Fairly, data protection/GDPR in one section, vulnerable customers.
11. **Ethics, the broker's day job & how the market talks** — professionalism, conflicts of interest, commission disclosure, demands & needs, suitability, documentation, what a broker's week looks like (renewals, new business, claims advocacy, market relationships), glossary of 40 terms she'll hear in a broking office (TOBA, slip, binder, MGA, bordereau, rate, loss ratio, run-off, etc.).
12. **Health & protection insurance** — PMI (individual and group/corporate), what it typically covers and excludes (pre-existing conditions, chronic vs acute, moratorium vs full medical underwriting), cash plans, dental, income protection, critical illness, life cover, group risk for employers, how health broking works (employee benefits consulting, renewals, claims support, wellbeing add-ons), NHS vs private interface, why a clinical background is an advantage in this space. Keep it an overview — no deep pricing or underwriting mechanics.

## Question bank rules (apply to every agent)

- Multiple choice, **4 options, exactly one correct**. No "all of the above" / "none of the above".
- Mix: ~50% recall/definition, ~30% applied scenario ("Poppy's friend's café floods…"), ~20% "which statement is true/false".
- Distractors must be plausible, not silly. Vary which position is correct.
- Each question has a 1–2 sentence **explanation** that teaches, not just "A is correct".
- Each question is tagged with `module` and a short `topic` string.
- No two questions in a module may test the identical fact with reworded stems.
- Cross-module exam questions (the extra ~40) should combine concepts, e.g. proximate cause + exclusions, fair presentation + remedies, indemnity + average.

## Data schema

Store content as JSON under `src/content/`, validated by `scripts/validate.mjs` (run in CI and before build; fail the build on any error).

```jsonc
// src/content/modules/01-what-insurance-is.json
{
  "id": "m01",
  "order": 1,
  "title": "What insurance actually is",
  "estimatedMinutes": 15,
  "recap": ["...", "...", "...", "...", "..."],
  "sections": [
    { "heading": "...", "body": "markdown string" }
  ],
  "whyItMatters": "one or two sentences",
  "questions": [
    {
      "id": "m01-q01",
      "topic": "peril vs hazard",
      "stem": "...",
      "options": ["...", "...", "...", "..."],
      "answerIndex": 2,
      "explanation": "..."
    }
  ]
}
```

- `src/content/exam-extra.json` holds the ~40 cross-module questions in the same question shape with `"module": "mixed"`.
- Validator checks: 12 modules, unique ids, exactly 30 questions per module, 4 options, `answerIndex` in range, non-empty explanation, body word count 1,600–2,400, recap has 5 items, exam bank total ≥ 250 after dedupe on normalised stem.

## App

- **Vite + React + TypeScript**, no backend, no auth. Tailwind or plain CSS modules — keep dependencies minimal.
- **Mobile-first** (design at 375px, must also look fine on desktop). Large tap targets, readable type (≥16px body), generous line height, dark-mode via `prefers-color-scheme`.
- Routes (hash router so GitHub Pages works without 404 config): `#/` home, `#/module/:id` reading, `#/module/:id/quiz`, `#/exam`, `#/exam/results`, `#/progress`.
- Home: progress ring/bar, module list with locked/available/passed state and best score, final exam card (locked until all passed), "Continue where you left off" button.
- Reading view: sections rendered from markdown, sticky "Take the quiz" button, remember scroll position per module.
- Quiz view: one question at a time, progress dots, immediate feedback, can't change an answer once submitted, score screen with retry/next.
- Exam view: one question at a time, "flag for review" and a review grid before submitting, autosaves in-progress answers so a closed tab doesn't lose the attempt, elapsed timer, results by module.
- **Progress in `localStorage`** under one key `poppy-insurance-progress-v1`:
  ```ts
  {
    version: 1,
    modules: { [moduleId]: { passed: boolean, bestScore: number, attempts: number, lastAttemptAt: string, scrollPos?: number } },
    exam: { attempts: { startedAt: string, finishedAt: string, score: number, byModule: Record<string, {correct: number, total: number}> }[], inProgress?: {...} },
    lastVisited?: string
  }
  ```
- `#/progress` page: attempts history, plus **Export** (copies a compact base64 string) and **Import** (paste) so she can move between phone and laptop. Also a "Reset everything" with confirm.
- Small, friendly touches: a one-line encouragement on pass ("Nice — 9/10. On to the market."), confetti-free. No sound.
- Accessibility: keyboard-operable, focus states, aria-live for feedback.
